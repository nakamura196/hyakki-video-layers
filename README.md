# Hyakki Yagyō with video layers

> **AI-generated content, modified version.** All 19 clips were generated by AI in Google Flow from crops of the image: 15 with Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash and 4 (003, 011, 017, 018) with Veo 3.1 Fast. Their motion, sound and any details not in the picture are the AI's invention: they are not part of the original scroll, and they are not a scholarly reconstruction. The image of the original itself is not altered; it is loaded from the IIIF Image API of The University of Tokyo. To see or cite the original, use the [original manifest](https://da.dl.itc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/portal/repo/iiif/fbd0479b-dbb4-4eaa-95b8-f27e1c423e4b/manifest) or the [item page](https://da.dl.itc.u-tokyo.ac.jp/portal/assets/fbd0479b-dbb4-4eaa-95b8-f27e1c423e4b).

A IIIF Presentation 3.0 manifest of the handscroll *Hyakki Yagyō-zu* (百鬼夜行図, The University of Tokyo General Library), with 19 short AI-generated clips that make some of its figures move.

The manifest has the same shape as IIIF Cookbook recipe [0036 Composition from Multiple Images](https://iiif.io/api/cookbook/recipe/0036-composition-from-multiple-images/): a base image, plus more resources painted onto regions of the Canvas with `#xywh=`. Here each added resource is a `Video` instead of an `Image`. The Canvas has no `duration`. Each video's `summary` and `metadata` say that it is AI-generated, and keep the motion prompt used to generate it.

- Manifest: https://nakamura196.github.io/hyakki-video-layers/manifest.json
- View in IIIF Kasane: https://kasane.ldas.jp/en/viewer?manifest=https%3A%2F%2Fnakamura196.github.io%2Fhyakki-video-layers%2Fmanifest.json&tab=layers

## Contents

- `manifest.json`: the manifest
- `videos/`: the clips (MP4, 19 files, about 124 MB)
- `scripts/convert.py`: makes `manifest.json` from the manifest of the original IIIF Animated Viewer (where the videos were in `canvas.annotations` with a describing text body)
- `scripts/enrich.py`: adds the descriptive metadata (labels and summary in Japanese and English, the original scroll, the clips, attribution, thumbnail) and short names for the clips, and measures each clip's size and duration with ffprobe. Run it after `convert.py`
- `scripts/prompts.json`: the model and the motion prompt (English, and a Japanese translation) of each clip; `enrich.py` reads it
- `scripts/blend.py`, `scripts/motionmask.py`: the post-processing (below). They take the clip downloaded from Flow and the original crop

How the image was modified:

1. Regions with figures were cropped from the IIIF image.
2. Each crop was given to Google Flow as both the start frame and the end frame, with a motion prompt, to make an 8-second clip. The prompts were written by Claude (Anthropic) for each scene from the crop: they name each figure by its place and clothing, and ask it to move clearly without turning into another figure or adding new ones.
3. Post-processing (`blend.py`, then `motionmask.py`): each clip was trimmed back to its region and colour-matched to the original. The edge band (24 px, blended over 96 px) was replaced with the pixels of the original, so there is no seam. Then only the parts that move were taken from the video: the pixels whose blurred brightness deviates from the clip's temporal median by more than 6, grown by 24 px and feathered by 32 px (one mask per clip). Everything else is the original pixels, so the still paper and lines do not flicker.
4. The clips were placed back on the regions they came from (`#xywh`).

Most clips were made with Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash. Omni refused three crops (003, 011, 018) under Google's policy on sexual content, probably because of the nearly naked figures, and for one (017) it ignored the start frame twice. Those four were made with Veo 3.1 Fast. **In the Veo clips the first and last frames match the original, but in between the figures are redrawn in a different style with bolder lines and their layout changes; things not in the picture appear and some figures disappear.** Each of them has a note in its metadata. Even in the Omni clips, an object sometimes leaves a faint double where it moved, because the mask's feathered band mixes the moved figure with the original one.
